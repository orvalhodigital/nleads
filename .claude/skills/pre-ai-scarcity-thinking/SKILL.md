# Pre-AI Scarcity Thinking

Goal: detect when business, career, or process advice is valuing the old task layer instead of the current bottleneck.

## Core rule

If the advice assumes humans should remain the default engine for routine clerical cognition, it is probably pre-AI scarcity thinking.

Sharper rule:

- do not ask whether a human can do the task for a wage
- ask what still stays scarce once AI makes the routine layer cheap

## Common symptoms

- recommending manual clerical skill depth as the primary moat
- treating AI as a small productivity aid rather than a labor-market shift
- assuming reconciliation, classification, drafting, or reporting remain scarce human value
- advising people to become the execution layer instead of the control, judgment, or exception layer
- designing orgs and processes around yesterday's cost structure

## Review process

1. State what advice is being given.
2. Identify what layer of work the advice assumes is scarce.
3. Check whether AI likely compresses that layer.
4. Identify what bottlenecks remain scarce.
5. Decide whether the advice is task-centric or bottleneck-centric.
6. Rewrite the advice for the current capability frontier.

## Output
### Advice being reviewed
### Assumed scarce resource
### Likely AI-compressed work
### Still-scarce bottlenecks
### Task being valued vs bottleneck that matters
### Why the advice lags
### Recommended correction
